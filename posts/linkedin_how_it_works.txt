I got an AI that can't write or draw to paint a Bob Ross landscape.

It's paint-by-numbers. My code makes the numbered sheet. The AI picks the paint for every spot. 35 seconds. 24 cents. The video replays it at 4x speed.

The AI is Jev, TypeSafe's decision model on OpenRouter. Jev does one thing. It answers multiple-choice questions and says how sure it is. No text. No images.

Here is how the painting works.

1. Jev plans the picture. I ask eight questions. Day or sunset? Where does the mountain go? A cabin or not? Jev chose a bright clear day (69% sure), two or three happy little clouds (94%), and a second mountain, because everybody needs a friend (72%).

2. My code draws the sheet. It works out where the mountain, trees and lake go. Jev never sees a picture.

3. Jev paints. I ask about a big square first: "Is this square all one colour?" If yes, Jev names the paint and the whole square goes on. If no, I cut it in four and ask again. The sky takes a few big strokes. Only the edges go pixel by pixel.

The result: 512 x 512 pixels from 37,856 decisions. 99.81% of pixels right. The wrong picks show up as tiny specks, like brush slips.

Why I think Jev is cool:
- It's fast. 37,856 decisions in 35 seconds.
- It's cheap. $0.042 per million input tokens, and the answers are free.
- It tells you how sure it is, every single time.
- It answers only what you ask. No essays.

The honest part: Jev did not imagine the mountain. The shapes came from my code. Jev chose the composition and made every colour choice. Give it only a description in words and it paints a blur.

Painting is a toy. But plenty of software makes thousands of small decisions a day: routing, triage, sorting, checking. A model like this fits there.
